# Method in Action: Klarna's AI Assistant and the Return of Human Service (2024–2025)

> *Example for the [human-ai-role-redesign](../SKILL.md) skill.*

Klarna is the most visible public case of an agent absorbing a large share of a role's tasks. Within fifteen months the company publicly changed course and redesigned the human role around what the agent could not do. The record is public on both ends, which makes it useful for running this skill backwards: what the Process would have demanded at launch, and what Klarna ended up building.

**Primary sources.**
- Klarna press release, 27 February 2024: https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/
- CX Dive, "Klarna changes its AI tune and again recruits humans for customer service," 9 May 2025, reporting a Klarna spokesperson's emailed statements and CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski's interview with Bloomberg: https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/

Quotes below are attributed exactly as those sources attribute them. Internal Klarna KPIs, headcounts, and job descriptions are not public, so the steps note where the record is silent instead of filling it in.

## The facts

**February 2024 (Klarna press release).** After one month live, the OpenAI-powered assistant had handled "2.3 million conversations, two-thirds of Klarna's customer service chats," was "doing the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents," was "on par with human agents in regard to customer satisfaction score," produced "a 25% drop in repeat inquiries," cut resolution time to "less than 2 mins compared to 11 mins previously," and was "estimated to drive a $40 million USD in profit improvement" in 2024. The release adds that "customers can still choose to interact with live agents if they'd prefer." It says nothing about what the human agents' roles would become.

**May 2025 (CX Dive, citing Bloomberg and Klarna).** Klarna now wanted customers always to have the option of a human. Siemiatkowski told Bloomberg: "As cost unfortunately seems to have been a too predominant evaluation factor when organizing this, what you end up having is lower quality," and "Really investing in the quality of the human support is the way of the future for us." Klarna was recruiting for what Siemiatkowski called an "Uber-type" customer-service setup — a pilot with remote work and "competitive pay and full flexibility," per spokesperson Clare Nordstrom. Nordstrom described the split: "AI solves the easy stuff — our experts handle the moments that matter," for "a new kind of role that blends frontline excellence with real-time product feedback." The assistant still handled two-thirds of inquiries.

## Running the steps

**1. Decompose into tasks.** The 2024 release itself lists the task inventory the assistant covered: "refunds, returns, payment-related issues, cancellations, disputes, and invoice inaccuracies," plus balance and payment-schedule questions, in 35+ languages. That is a task-level decomposition — but only of what the agent took. *Gate:* nothing public shows the residual human tasks (complex disputes, distressed customers, edge cases) being listed with their share of hours. The 90% coverage gate would have forced that list.

**2. Assign destinations.** De facto: routine errands → agent; everything else → human, with "live agents if they'd prefer" as the fallback. The 2025 statement makes the destination explicit — "AI solves the easy stuff — our experts handle the moments that matter." That is step 2 done after the fact.

**3. Write the new human duties.** The 2025 role names two of the skill's four categories outright: **judgment and exception handling** ("the moments that matter") and **feedback to the system** ("real-time product feedback," part of supervising the agent). **Relationships** reappear as the brand promise that "there will be always a human if you want." *Gate:* in 2024 no human duty or owner was announced alongside the agent. In 2025 the duty was defined first and the recruiting followed.

**4. Rewrite the KPIs.** The 2024 scorecard was throughput and cost: conversations handled, FTE-equivalents, minutes to resolution, profit impact. Satisfaction was reported as parity, not as a target. By the CEO's own account, cost was "a too predominant evaluation factor" and quality fell. This is exactly the failure the step 4 gate stops: a volume-and-cost KPI left as the dominant measure fights the quality the human role exists to supply. Klarna's 2025 language ("investing in the quality of the human support") moves the target to outcome and quality.

**5. Close skill gaps.** The pilot recruited "highly educated students, professionals and entrepreneurs" (Nordstrom). Klarna redefined the skill profile for the residual role — complex, judgment-heavy contacts — instead of keeping the old frontline profile. Training content and pass criteria are not public.

**6. Align incentives.** "Competitive pay and full flexibility to attract the best" is an explicit incentive rule for the redesigned role. Per Siemiatkowski (via Bloomberg), one goal was to replace thousands of outsourced workers — a stated destination for the work, which step 6 requires.

**7. Communicate the change.** Klarna's 2025 messaging follows the step's order: why ("AI gives us speed. Talent gives us empathy"), what stays the same (the assistant still handles two-thirds of inquiries), and the new path (a pilot for a new kind of role). CX Dive characterizes the 2024 period as one in which the firm "laid off workers and paused hiring." In 2024, no public role path came with the agent's launch.

**8. Stop-rule.** The reversal is the stop-rule firing late. Once quality loss showed that cost savings were not the right destination for the freed capacity, the question went back to the business level — what service model, and what human role — and the role was rebuilt.

## The mapped steps

1. **Tasks:** agent task list public (refunds, returns, disputes, invoices…); residual human tasks never listed in 2024.
2. **Destinations:** routine → agent; "moments that matter" → human (made explicit only in 2025).
3. **New duties:** judgment / exceptions, product feedback, "always a human" relationship — defined in 2025.
4. **KPIs:** 2024 scorecard = volume + cost; CEO: cost "too predominant," "lower quality" → gate violated.
5. **Skills:** new profile recruited for complex contacts.
6. **Incentives:** competitive pay + flexibility; insourcing outsourced work.
7. **Communication:** why / what stays / new path — in 2025.
8. **Stop-rule:** quality loss sent the decision back to the business level; role redesigned.

**The lesson:** Klarna's agent metrics were real. The gap was that the human role was never rewritten when the agent launched, so cost stayed the scorecard. The skill's order — duties and KPIs first, agent second — is the redesign Klarna ended up doing in reverse.
