# Sources — human-ai-role-redesign

> *Primary sources for the [human-ai-role-redesign](../SKILL.md) skill.*

- **Autor, D. H., Levy, F., & Murnane, R. J.** (2003). "The Skill Content of Recent Technological Change: An Empirical Exploration." *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 118(4), 1279–1333. **Primary source for the task-based view**: computerization substitutes for routine tasks and complements non-routine ones, changing jobs at the task level rather than eliminating whole occupations. https://doi.org/10.1162/003355303322552801 · https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/118/4/1279/1925105
- **Brynjolfsson, E., Li, D., & Raymond, L. R.** (2023). "Generative AI at Work." NBER Working Paper 31161. **5,179 customer-support agents; a generative AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 14% on average and 34% for novice and low-skilled workers, with minimal impact on experienced workers.** https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161
- **Dell'Acqua, F., McFowland III, E., Mollick, E., Lifshitz-Assaf, H., Kellogg, K. C., Rajendran, S., Krayer, L., Candelon, F., & Lakhani, K. R.** (2023). "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality." Harvard Business School Working Paper 24-013; *Organization Science* (2026). **758 BCG consultants; inside the frontier AI users completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, at higher quality; on a task outside the frontier they were 19% less likely to produce a correct solution.** https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700
- **O\*NET OnLine** (U.S. Department of Labor, Employment & Training Administration). Task-level occupation descriptions (19,000+ occupation-specific task statements) usable for step 1 decomposition. https://www.onetonline.org/
- **Kotter, J. P.** (1995). "Leading Change: Why Transformation Efforts Fail." *Harvard Business Review*, March–April 1995; expanded as *Leading Change* (Harvard Business School Press, 1996). Change-communication grounding for step 7. https://hbr.org/1995/05/leading-change-why-transformation-efforts-fail-2
- **Klarna.** "Klarna AI assistant handles two-thirds of customer service chats in its first month." Press release, 27 February 2024. **Primary source for the case's 2024 metrics** (2.3M conversations, 700 FTE-equivalent, satisfaction parity, 25% fewer repeat inquiries, <2 vs 11 minutes, $40M profit estimate). https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/
- **Doerer, K.** "Klarna changes its AI tune and again recruits humans for customer service." *CX Dive*, 9 May 2025. **Source for the 2025 redesign**: Klarna spokesperson Clare Nordstrom's emailed statements and CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski's quotes from his Bloomberg interview (cost "a too predominant evaluation factor," "lower quality," "Uber-type" setup). https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/

## What is NOT cited and why

- The four human-duty categories (judgment, exception handling, relationships, agent supervision), the 20% and 90% thresholds, and the KPI shift pattern are **deciqAI synthesis**, not a published standard.
- The Bloomberg interview itself is paywalled; Siemiatkowski's words are cited as CX Dive reports them. A widely circulated line — "Initially, Klarna embraced AI with an eye toward cost savings… but perhaps underestimated the tradeoff" — comes from analyst Julie Geller (Info-Tech Research Group) in that article, **not** from Siemiatkowski, and is not used here as a Klarna statement.
