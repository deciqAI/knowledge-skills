# Sources — agent-delegability-assessment

> *Primary sources for the [agent-delegability-assessment](../SKILL.md) skill. All URLs checked October 2026.*

- **Parasuraman, R., Sheridan, T. B., & Wickens, C. D. (2000).** "A model for types and levels of human interaction with automation." *IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics — Part A: Systems and Humans*, 30(3), 286–297. Automation is applied at levels across information-processing stages, and the level is chosen by evaluating the consequences. This is the basis for the L1–L4 scale. https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354
- **Sheridan, T. B., & Verplank, W. L. (1978).** *Human and Computer Control of Undersea Teleoperators.* MIT Man-Machine Systems Laboratory, technical report for the Office of Naval Research (NTIS accession ADA057655). The original 10-level scale of automation. https://ntrl.ntis.gov/NTRL/dashboard/searchResults/titleDetail/ADA057655.xhtml
- **Bainbridge, L. (1983).** "Ironies of automation." *Automatica*, 19(6), 775–779. Automating a task leaves the operator the hardest job, monitoring and intervening, while eroding the skills that job needs. Source for "keep the human's hand in" and for scheduled spot checks at L4. https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8
- **Anthropic (2024). "Building effective agents."** Agents "can then pause for human feedback at checkpoints or when encountering blockers." Autonomy brings "higher costs, and the potential for compounding errors," and Anthropic recommends "extensive testing in sandboxed environments, along with the appropriate guardrails." https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents
- **NIST (2023). *AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*, NIST AI 100-1, released January 26, 2023.** A voluntary framework covering human oversight and risk-proportionate controls (GOVERN / MAP / MEASURE / MANAGE). https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- ***Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024 BCCRT 149 (February 14, 2024).** Primary source for the case study and for the rationalization row: a chatbot "is still just a part of Air Canada's website"; the company is responsible "whether the information comes from a static page or a chatbot." https://decisions.civilresolutionbc.ca/crt/crtd/en/item/525448/index.do · summary: https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2024-february/bc-tribunal-confirms-companies-remain-liable-information-provided-ai-chatbot/

**What is NOT cited and why:** the six scoring dimensions, the 20% exception-rate cap and the level-mapping rules are deciqAI's synthesis for SMB operators, not a published standard. The L1–L4 compression of the Sheridan–Verplank 10-level scale is likewise deciqAI's. The scoring of the Air Canada task in the case study is a reconstruction from the tribunal record, which does not describe the chatbot's configuration.
