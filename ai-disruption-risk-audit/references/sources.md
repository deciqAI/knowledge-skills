# Sources — ai-disruption-risk-audit

> *Primary sources for the [ai-disruption-risk-audit](../SKILL.md) skill.*

## Frameworks the audit builds on

- **Porter, Michael E.** *Competitive Advantage: Creating and Sustaining Superior Performance.* Free Press, 1985. **Primary source for the value chain as the unit of analysis (Step 1).**
- **Christensen, Clayton M.** *The Innovator's Dilemma.* Harvard Business School Press, 1997. **Primary source for disruptive innovation — low-end and new-market entry by cheaper, "good enough" offerings.**
- **Christensen, Clayton M. & Raynor, Michael E.** *The Innovator's Solution.* Harvard Business School Press, 2003. **Primary source for the "law of conservation of attractive profits"** — when one layer of a value chain commoditizes, profit migrates to an adjacent layer (the basis of Step 5).

## Evidence that AI's impact is task-level and uneven

- **Eloundou, T., Manning, S., Mishkin, P. & Rock, D.** (2023). "GPTs are GPTs: An Early Look at the Labor Market Impact Potential of Large Language Models." arXiv:2303.10130. **Primary source for scoring exposure task by task rather than job by job** — the method Steps 2–3 borrow. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130
- **Brynjolfsson, E., Li, D. & Raymond, L. R.** (2023). "Generative AI at Work." NBER Working Paper 31161. **Primary field evidence (5,179 customer-support agents) that generative AI raised productivity 14% on average, with large differences across workers.** https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161
- **Dell'Acqua, F., McFowland III, E., Mollick, E., et al.** (2023). "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier." Harvard Business School Working Paper 24-013; later published in *Organization Science* 37(2), 2026, https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838. **Primary source for the "jagged technology frontier" — AI improves some tasks and worsens others of similar apparent difficulty** (Overview). https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700
- **Stanford HAI.** *AI Index Report* (annual). **Primary source for tracking model capability and inference-cost trends when re-scoring every 6 months.** https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index

## What is NOT cited and why

- The scoring dimensions (substitutability, cost collapse, customer bypass), the red-zone rule and the hold / attack / retreat classification are a deciqAI practice synthesis. The task-level approach borrows from Eloundou et al., but the scores must come from the user's own tests.
- The Chegg scores in the example are an illustrative retrospective reading of public filings, not figures Chegg published.
- The books above are cited as books (no stable free full-text URL); the arXiv, NBER and HBS links point to the papers' official pages.
