# Proprietary Data Moat

## Overview

In the AI era, models and compute can be bought. What can become an advantage is **data others cannot get that makes the product better the more it is used** — and most claimed "data moats" fail that test. Casado & Lauten (a16z, 2019): most data defensibility "actually boil[s] down to scale effects," and "the cost of adding unique data to your corpus may actually go up, while the value of incremental data goes down." Hagiu & Wright (*HBR*, 2020): people "grossly overestimate the advantage that data confers"; it holds only when the data offers high and lasting value, is proprietary, and leads to improvements that cannot easily be imitated.

Core mechanism: **inventory the data → pass uniqueness and compounding tests → design the feedback loop → secure rights and quality → turn it into an advantage the customer can feel.** Rights are a hard gate, not an afterthought: regulators have ordered companies to delete models trained on improperly obtained data (FTC, *Everalbum*, 2021).

Compose with [economic-moat](../economic-moat/SKILL.md) (moat types) · [network-effects](../network-effects/SKILL.md) (telling data scale effects apart from true network effects) · [feedback-loops](../feedback-loops/SKILL.md) (the flywheel) · [switching-costs](../switching-costs/SKILL.md) (data lock-in).

## When to Use

- The product depends on AI and you need to know whether its differentiation is durable
- Fundraising or strategy work raises the question of data defensibility
- Deciding whether to invest in data collection, labeling, or data partnerships

**When NOT to use:** the data has no product use · pure engineering or tooling choice · data ownership is unclear — resolve rights and compliance before discussing a moat.

## The Process

**Output:** Data moat assessment card

1. **Inventory the data.** For each data class: source, volume, update frequency, and how it arises (product use / manual collection / purchased / public).
2. **Uniqueness test.** Could competitors or general-model vendors buy it, scrape it, synthesize it, or substitute public data? *Gate: obtainable → not a moat; downgrade to "operating asset" → stop for this class.*
3. **Compounding test.** Does new data keep making the product better, or do marginal gains saturate quickly? *Gate: gains have saturated or cannot be measured → exclude from the moat.*
4. **Design the feedback loop.** Use → label or outcome signal → improved model or rules → better experience → more use. Name the signal and how fast it flows back. *Gate: the signal needs heavy manual work → estimate unit cost; redesign if too high.*
5. **Rights and consent.** Do contracts and the privacy policy permit use for training and improvement? Is cross-customer use authorized? Which regimes apply (GDPR, CCPA, etc.)? *Gate: no clear right → exclude the data until authorization is obtained.*
6. **Quality.** Accuracy, coverage, freshness, bias; assign owners for sampling and cleaning.
7. **Productize.** Which customer-visible improvement does the data produce (accuracy, speed, personalization, benchmarks), and how is it measured?
8. **Stop-rule:** no data class passes steps 2, 3, and 5 together → concede there is no data moat and compete on distribution, workflow, or brand instead.

**Output template:**
`data class | source | uniqueness (obtainable?) | compounding (measurable lift?) | loop signal + cycle time | rights basis | quality metric | product advantage + measure | verdict (moat / operating asset / drop)`

## Data Packs

Typical patterns (strongest data class / main uniqueness threat / rights question / dominant failure):
- **Vertical workflow SaaS:** outcome labels from customers' own processes / customers can take their data elsewhere / cross-customer training rights / counting raw logs as a moat.
- **Marketplaces:** transaction and match outcomes / multi-homing users carry the same signal to rivals / data-sharing terms with both sides / confusing liquidity (a network effect) with data.
- **Consumer apps:** behavioral and content data at scale / general-model vendors can approximate it / consent for training, especially biometric or sensitive data / default-on collection.
- **Enterprise AI tools:** customer feedback and corrections / contract bars use beyond the customer / per-customer versus pooled learning / promising "we never train on your data" and doing it anyway.

## Applying It Well

1. **Narrow and deep beats big and broad.** Proprietary outcomes from a specific industry workflow are often worth more than a large generic corpus.
2. **Outcome labels are scarcer than raw logs.** "Did it close?" and "was it resolved?" are what competitors cannot scrape.
3. **Build consent and rights into the product,** not into a cleanup project later.
4. **Separate scale effects from network effects.** A model that improves with more data is not the same as a product that is more valuable because other users are on it.

## Red Flags

- Cannot say why a competitor could not get the data · no measured lift · training use has no contractual basis · nobody owns data quality · the moat exists only in the pitch deck
