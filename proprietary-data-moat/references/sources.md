# Sources — proprietary-data-moat

> *Primary sources for the [proprietary-data-moat](../SKILL.md) skill.*

- **Casado, M., & Lauten, P.** (9 May 2019). "The Empty Promise of Data Moats." Andreessen Horowitz. **Most data defensibility "boil[s] down to scale effects"; "the cost of adding unique data to your corpus may actually go up, while the value of incremental data goes down"; skepticism that enterprise data network effects exist in practice.** https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/
- **Hagiu, A., & Wright, J.** (2020). "When Data Creates Competitive Advantage." *Harvard Business Review*, January–February 2020. **People "grossly overestimate the advantage that data confers"; data-enabled learning defends only when the data offers high and lasting value, is proprietary, leads to improvements that can't be easily imitated, or generates insights that can be quickly incorporated** — the basis for steps 2–3. https://hbr.org/2020/01/when-data-creates-competitive-advantage
- **Sambasivan, N., Kapania, S., Highfill, H., Akrong, D., Paritosh, P., & Aroyo, L.** (2021). "'Everyone wants to do the model work, not the data work': Data Cascades in High-Stakes AI." *CHI '21*. **53 practitioners; data cascades "pervasive (92% prevalence), invisible, delayed, but often avoidable"** — the basis for step 6. https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445518 · https://research.google/pubs/everyone-wants-to-do-the-model-work-not-the-data-work-data-cascades-in-high-stakes-ai/
- **U.S. Federal Trade Commission, Tech@FTC.** (9 January 2024). "AI Companies: Uphold Your Privacy and Confidentiality Commitments." **Warns model-as-a-service firms against using customer data for undisclosed training or quietly changing terms; notes the FTC has required deletion of "models and algorithms developed in whole or in part using that unlawfully obtained data."** https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/01/ai-companies-uphold-your-privacy-confidentiality-commitments
- **U.S. Federal Trade Commission.** *In the Matter of Everalbum, Inc.*, File No. 192 3172 (proposed January 2021; final May 2021). **Primary source for the worked example**: default-on facial recognition, datasets built from millions of user facial images, order to delete photos, face embeddings, and models/algorithms. Case page: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/192-3172-everalbum-inc-matter · Press release: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2021/01/california-company-settles-ftc-allegations-it-deceived-consumers-about-use-facial-recognition-photo · Final: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2021/05/ftc-finalizes-settlement-photo-app-developer-related-misuse-facial-recognition-technology
- **Regulation (EU) 2016/679** (General Data Protection Regulation). Named in step 5 as an applicable regime. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng
- **California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)**, California Office of the Attorney General. Consumer rights to know, delete, and opt out; named in step 5. https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa

## What is NOT cited and why

- The three-test structure (uniqueness / compounding / rights), the assessment card, and the stop-rule are **deciqAI practice synthesis**, drawing on Casado & Lauten's and Hagiu & Wright's criteria; they are not a published standard.
- The Everalbum facts are FTC allegations resolved by consent order, not adjudicated findings; the example states them as such.
